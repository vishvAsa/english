Silence  
After the great storm,  
the trees beside the rock  
stood still  
in pin-drop silence.  
The clouds had vanished;  
the birds were gone.  
The sky gazed down  
with its hollow eyes.  
The morning sun soaked the woods  
in golden light,  
its rays reaching toward the horizons.  
This was not the silence of melancholy.  
Not the silence of nirvāṇa.  
Not the numbness that follows pain.  
It was simply silence.  
Perhaps waiting  
for some seeds  
to find their footing in the earth.  
Perhaps watching itself  
unfold  
in its own soundless rhythm.  

The rock stood still—  
not waiting to move,  
yet not held  
in the grip of inertia.  
For nothing is inert.  

Some things  
are simply silent. 

Some send out echoes;  
others absorb the songs.  
This, too, is silence—  
not the absence of movement,  
but movement without a sound.  
An active silence.  
I can feel it—  
not in words,  
not in thoughts,  
but in that moment  
when silence,  
held as thought,  
dissolves.
