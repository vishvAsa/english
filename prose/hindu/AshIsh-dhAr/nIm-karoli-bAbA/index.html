NIm karoli bAbA

I don’t remember when I first heard of Neem Karoli Baba. It must’ve been a long time ago, probably when I was in school or college. But it was sometime in 2017 in a workshop on Dharmashastras conducted by Dr. Bharat Gupt that I met someone who had met Babaji in person. It was the French-American scholar, the late Dr. Yvette Rosser, better known by her Hindu name Ram Rani. She talked about how her life was transformed by his grace and narrated more than one ‘miracle’ stories of how Baba was still guiding her even after he had passed on many decades ago.

Honestly, I have no taste for miracle stories - not that I don’t believe that miracles happen but I just find no use for them, especially in the age of Insta-babas and reel-devis. But I could see Ram Rani ji’s eyes light up every time she said “Baba”.

In a parallel universe sometime before she made the serious error of judgement of marrying me, my wife visited Baba’s ashram in Rishikesh and was told that the real ashram was somewhere up in the mountains at this remote weird-sounding village called Kainchi. She decided that she would go there one day. She got busy with life and only remembered her sankalpa more than a decade later.

I called Ram Rani ji and requested her to pull some strings and get us a room as close to the temple as possible. She arranged for us to stay literally at the first room after crossing the temple threshold. There were no homestays, no geysers, no heaters, no room service, no fancy restaurants. And there were more monkeys than I’d ask for if Nature’s intent was only to remind you of Hanuman’s vanar sena. It was January and the cold was so extreme that my wife almost fainted while attending the morning aarti at the temple. There were no other visitors. We sat and recited Hanuman Chalisa, ate daal chawal, roamed around the village. The trip was truly memorable. I carried back a picture of Neem Karoli Baba from the ashram, mesmerised by the disarming smile on his radiant face.

After a couple of years, some big celebrities from America visited Kainchi one after another and this generated tremendous spiritual yearning among travel vloggers, many of whom made it their life mission to improve the GDP of Nainital district. Predictably, their followers began thronging the sleepy village like never before. Within three years or so, they successfully transformed Kainchi into a big tourist destination with a parking lot the size of two football fields, restaurants, cafes, luxury homestays, and traffic jams that last more than the collective attention span of Instagram’s spiritual community.

The temple visits became mechanical. Even the monkeys disappeared, and rather than feeling euphoric about the rising footfalls like a good Hindu who celebrates development and progress, I felt sad about it like a Luddite - a crime that no amount of word-celling can whitewash. I don’t know, I felt that the disappearance of monkeys was a sign that the spirit of the place was amiss and I may not be able to find it again in this lifetime. The spirit of Kainchi is of course the feeling of being in Neem Karoli Baba’s presence.

I was wrong. Last month, the awesome @SUKETDHIR_ , one of the very few legit celebrities who don’t act like celebrities, called me and asked me to come to watch the special screening of a film on Neem Karoli Baba in Aerocity. I was in two minds because I don’t generally like the treatment of Dharmic subjects in films these days.

But then he mentioned that the director was @filmervishal . I knew Vishal was a serious devotee and a medical doctor who passionately pursued filmmaking as a craft. I asked my wife if she wanted to go and she looked at me as if she intended to book me under the SC/ST Act. “Of course we are going! What are you even thinking?” I am glad we took our son along too.

Hanuman Ansh is a steady-paced, no-frills, emotionally gripping experience that calmly assures you that taking birth on this sacred land - despite its cynical politics, crumbling infrastructure, systemic corruption and environmental destruction - is a spiritual blessing that outlasts the material suffering of many lifetimes. The plot starts in the pre-independence era and ends with Baba’s settling in Kainchi.

The film traces his departure from home after losing his mother followed by decades of intense tapasya at different places across the sacred geography. Of course, there are miracles that happen along the way but Vishal has weaved them into the narrative with great sensitivity and craftmanship, rather than the over-the-top spiritual slop you get in big-budget embarrassments and small-budget cringe. A labour of love in the true sense of the word, you can see that the entire crew has poured their heart into the film.

The best part is that Vishal has managed to put it up on the big screen in many cities. Do check for shows in your city on Bookmyshow and watch it before it is gone. I guess, for Vishal, the effort was its own reward, but here’s our chance to share the joy that he felt in making it. Take your family, friends and most importantly kids, along. Experience Neem Karoli Baba’s presence via the big screen. Jai Bajrang Bali.

PS: Vishal bhai, we are eagerly waiting for the sequel.
